# War of Expansion

## Theme & Tone
Dark satire of imperial overreach. Hubris → catastrophe arc. The story explores how a "quick victory" becomes a quagmire, with escalating costs and consequences.

## Key Characters
- **The Generals** - Hawkish, corrupt military leadership pushing for expansion
- **The Mercenary Boss** - Wildcard warlord with private army (Wagner-like)
- **Western Leaders** - Imposing sanctions and condemnation
- **The People** - Suffering from mobilization, casualties, economic collapse

## Story Beats

1. **Inciting Incident**: Generals propose a "72-hour operation" to annex neighboring territory. Intelligence promises easy victory.

2. **Escalation**: 
   - War stalls far beyond the promised timeline
   - Mobilization becomes necessary
   - Equipment failures and corruption exposed
   - Casualties mount, coffins return home

3. **Complications**:
   - War crimes allegations surface
   - Mercenary forces mutiny
   - International sanctions escalate
   - Economic collapse accelerates
   - Elite sons flee conscription

4. **Climax**: 
   - Nuclear brinkmanship (point of no return)
   - OR catastrophic military defeat
   - OR frozen conflict becomes permanent

5. **Resolution** (multiple paths):
   - **Frozen Conflict**: Permanent occupation, endless insurgency
   - **Negotiated Settlement**: Face-saving withdrawal, sanctions relief
   - **Total Defeat**: Military collapse, regime change
   - **Nuclear Escalation**: Apocalyptic consequences

## Branching Points

- **Launch vs Reject invasion** (major fork)
  - Launch → War events activate
  - Reject → Nationalist backlash, potential coup

- **Escalate vs Negotiate** (mid-story fork)
  - Escalate → More casualties, war crimes risk
  - Negotiate → Face-saving exit, but elite anger

- **Nuclear option** (point of no return)
  - Use → Pariah status, potential apocalypse
  - Threaten → Brinkmanship, credibility test
  - Reject → Generals lose faith, search for alternatives

- **Mercenary mutiny** (wildcard)
  - Negotiate → Temporary deal, future problems
  - Fight → Civil war risk
  - Eliminate → Ruthless reputation, fear-based control

## Design Notes

- Events should reflect the absurdity of "72-hour operation" becoming years-long conflict
- Choices should force tradeoffs: military success vs. economic stability vs. public support
- No "good" endings - all paths have severe consequences
- War profiteering should be tempting but ultimately destructive
- Elite hypocrisy (sons fleeing) should be a recurring theme
