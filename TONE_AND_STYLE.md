# Tone and Style Guide

## Core Tone

**The Oligarch's Gambit** employs dark satire to critique authoritarian power. The tone is:

- **Cynical but not nihilistic**: Shows corruption while maintaining player engagement
- **Sardonic humor**: Laughing at the absurdity of power
- **Grounded in reality**: Inspired by real events without being preachy
- **Gritty and unvarnished**: No sugarcoating the moral compromises

## Voice and Perspective

### Narrative Voice

The game uses **third-person omniscient narrator** with a knowing, slightly ironic tone:

**Examples:**
- ❌ "You are presented with a difficult choice."
- ✅ "Your generals present invasion plans. Intelligence says the operation will take 72 hours maximum."

- ❌ "The people are unhappy about corruption."
- ✅ "Your PR team is panicking - bread lines are getting longer and social media is exploding with anger."

### Player Position

The player is **complicit, not righteous**. They're not playing a hero trying to fix a broken system; they're playing an oligarch trying to maintain power and accumulate wealth.

**Key Principles:**
- No "good guy" options - all choices have moral compromise
- Personal enrichment is always possible but comes with costs
- The game doesn't judge, but consequences speak for themselves
- Player should feel the weight of their choices

## Writing Style

### Event Titles

**Format:** Short, punchy, evocative

**Good Examples:**
- "The Yacht Incident"
- "An Inconvenient Reporter"
- "The Price of Bread"
- "The Aluminum King's Ambition"

**Bad Examples:**
- "An Oligarch Has a Party on His Yacht and People Are Mad"
- "Journalist Investigation Problem"
- "Food Inflation Crisis Requires Response"

**Guidelines:**
- 2-6 words typically
- Intrigue over exposition
- Noun phrases often work well
- Avoid generic terms like "crisis" or "problem" when possible

### Event Descriptions

**Format:** 2-4 sentences, building tension

**Structure:**
1. **Setup**: What's happening
2. **Complication**: What makes this difficult
3. **Stakes**: What's at risk
4. **Optional detail**: Vivid specific that brings it to life

**Example:**

> "Your aluminum magnate friend just acquired a 500-foot superyacht with a submarine dock and missile defense system. He's hosting a party in the Mediterranean. Half your cabinet will be there with their mistresses. Your PR team is panicking - bread lines are getting longer and social media is exploding with anger."

**Breakdown:**
- Sentence 1: The yacht (vivid detail)
- Sentence 2: The party
- Sentence 3: Who's going (stakes)
- Sentence 4: The problem (complication)

### Choice Text

**Format:** 6-15 words, active voice, first person implied

**Good Examples:**
- "Launch the invasion. Annex their resources."
- "Eliminate her. Make it look like a robbery gone wrong."
- "Arrest him. Seize everything. Send him to Siberia."

**Bad Examples:**
- "I think we should probably consider launching an invasion"
- "Order the military to begin offensive operations"
- "The elimination option"

**Guidelines:**
- Imperative mood ("Do X" not "I will do X")
- Active, decisive verbs
- Specific actions, not vague intentions
- Multiple sentences are fine (creates rhythm)
- Include the "why" or consequence when relevant

### Numbers and Specificity

**Be specific** - Numbers create verisimilitude and dark humor:

**Good Examples:**
- "Your 23-year-old nephew just incorporated a 'consulting firm' last week"
- "Features include: aqua-disco, hookah lounge, underground ice hockey rink, casino, wine cellar for 10,000 bottles, and an $850,000 Italian toilet"
- "50,000 people are in the streets"

**Why it works:**
- Specificity is funny ("$850,000 Italian toilet")
- Numbers ground the absurdity in pseudo-reality
- Details show someone actually thought this through

## Satirical Techniques

### Juxtaposition

Place luxury and suffering side by side:

> "Total cost: $1.4 billion. The annual education budget is $800 million."

> "He's offering 40% of a $10 billion contract directly to your Cyprus account. His body armor fails ballistic tests."

### Euphemism and Doublespeak

Use the language of power:

**Examples:**
- "Special military operation" (not "invasion")
- "Pacification efforts" (not "crackdown")
- "Strategic communications" (not "propaganda")
- "Consulting fees" (not "bribes")
- "Disappear" (not "kill")
- "Liquidate" (not "murder")

But also **break the euphemism** when it's darkly funny:

> "Your FSB chief offers three options: novichok, a car accident, or we could just shoot her in the elevator."

### The Specific Detail

One concrete, absurd detail can carry an entire event:

- "Underground ice hockey rink"
- "Her 23-year-old nephew"
- "Polonium in his tea"
- "$850,000 Italian toilet"

### Understated Horror

Don't melodramatize the dark stuff - present it matter-of-factly:

**Example:**
> "Your GRU chief says they can 'handle it'—polonium, novichok, or a simple heart attack."

No exclamation points. No "evil laugh" moment. Just options, like ordering from a menu.

## What to Avoid

### Don't Be Preachy

❌ Bad:
> "This choice is morally wrong and will harm innocent people. Think about the consequences of authoritarianism."

✅ Good:
> "Pensioners are eating dog food. Social media is exploding. You could subsidize food (expensive), blame the West (classic), or arrest some supermarket CEOs for 'profiteering.'"

Let the situations speak for themselves.

### Don't Explain the Satire

❌ Bad:
> "Just like real dictators do, you can choose to rig the election."

✅ Good:
> "Your political technologist has the playbook ready: jail the main opponent on trumped-up charges, block other candidates for 'paperwork errors,' control all TV coverage..."

Trust the player to get it.

### Don't Make It Cartoonish

❌ Bad:
> "You are the most evil dictator ever! Muhahaha!"

✅ Good:
> "You're 68 years old. The oligarchs want to know: what happens when you die?"

Real power is banal and bureaucratic.

### Don't Offer "Good" Choices

❌ Bad:
> "A: Steal money, B: Be honest and virtuous"

✅ Good:
> "A: Route it through the nephew (big money, big risk), B: Take 10% and fill treasury (modest, safer)"

Both options involve corruption, just different flavors.

## Metric Changes

### Wealth Amounts

Numbers are now in **billions**:
- Personal wealth: $10B starting, up to $200B
- Treasury: $1000B starting, up to $2000B

### Effect Descriptions (Implied in choices)

Hint at effects in choice text when it helps:

> "Let it publish. Flood the zone with disinformation."
> (Implies: costs money, anger increases)

> "Arrest him. Seize everything. Send him to Siberia."
> (Implies: gain wealth, elite disapprove, public may react)

## Legacy Achievement Names

Legacy names should be:
- **Ironic**: "Democratic Champion (87%)"
- **Euphemistic**: "Liberator" (for invader)
- **Blunt**: "War Profiteer"
- **Darkly Humorous**: "Pirate King"
- **Self-Aware**: "Silencer"

**Format Patterns:**
- Titles: "The Ice King"
- Roles: "War Profiteer", "Palace Builder"
- Ironic honors: "Democratic Champion"
- Dark metaphors: "Long Reach", "Iron Fist"

## Cultural References

### Be Oblique

Don't name:
- Specific countries (Russia, Ukraine)
- Specific people (Putin, Navalny)
- Specific companies (Gazprom, Rosneft)

Do reference:
- "The West"
- "Nord Stream"
- "A neighboring territory"
- "Ethnic minorities"
- Recognizable situations

### Why This Works

1. **Legal safety**: Satire, not defamation
2. **Universal applicability**: Could be any autocrat
3. **Player complicity**: Harder to distance yourself
4. **Timelessness**: Won't date quickly

## Humor Balance

### Dark Humor Guidelines

**When to be darkly funny:**
- Absurd luxury juxtaposed with poverty
- Bureaucratic language for horrible acts
- Specific ridiculous details
- Self-serious autocratic behavior

**When to be serious:**
- Human cost should be real, not jokey
- Consequences of violence
- Metric changes (let numbers speak)

**Example of good balance:**

> "His body armor fails ballistic tests. His rations gave soldiers food poisoning. But he's offering 40% of a $10 billion contract directly to your Cyprus account."

- First two sentences: Serious consequences
- Third sentence: The absurd temptation

## Difficulty and Tone

### No Win States

There are no "happily ever after" scenarios:
- Staying in power requires corruption
- Accumulating wealth harms the state
- Every choice has costs
- Game over is inevitable

This reinforces the thematic point: authoritarian power is unsustainable and morally bankrupt.

### Grittier is Better

Recent update made the game grittier:
- Wealth in billions (emphasizes scale of theft)
- Harsher consequences
- More difficult to survive
- Legacy achievements weighted (not all positive)

Continue this direction - the game should feel **hard** and **morally uncomfortable**.

## Writing Process

### For Each Event

1. **Find the real inspiration**: What actual event/dilemma?
2. **Identify the core tension**: What's the impossible choice?
3. **Add specific details**: One or two vivid, concrete details
4. **Write matter-of-factly**: Present it as normal (it's not)
5. **Check metric effects**: Do consequences make sense?
6. **Read aloud**: Does it flow? Is timing right?

### For Storylines

1. **Start with the ending**: What's the big dramatic conclusion?
2. **Work backward**: How did we get here?
3. **Create branches**: What are 2-3 different paths?
4. **Add randomness**: What events could interrupt this?
5. **Check pacing**: Not too fast, not too slow
6. **Legacy payoff**: Is the conclusion satisfying?

## Final Checklist

Before adding an event, verify:

- [ ] Title is punchy (2-6 words)
- [ ] Description is 2-4 sentences
- [ ] At least one specific, vivid detail
- [ ] No "good" choices (all have costs)
- [ ] Choice text is active voice, decisive
- [ ] Metric changes make sense
- [ ] Tone is sardonic, not preachy
- [ ] No direct country/person names
- [ ] Legacy names (if any) are ironic or dark
- [ ] Event could be funny and serious simultaneously
- [ ] Player feels complicit, not heroic
